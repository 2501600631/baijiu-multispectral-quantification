## Figure 1

Incremental predictive performance relative to the fixed alcohol-strength baseline. Circles represent the spectral strategy selector; open squares represent the alcohol-assisted selector, which chooses among spectra-only, alcohol-only and alcohol-residual prediction routes using training data. Each point is 100 × (1 − RMSEstrategy/RMSEalcohol), calculated from the saved outer-fold predictions for 139 distinct bottles over five repetitions of five-fold evaluation. Positive values indicate smaller error than the fixed alcohol-only model (quadratic alcohol-strength features followed by Ridge regression, alpha = 1). Horizontal whiskers show the 2.5th–97.5th percentiles of 4,000 paired bottle-cluster resamples, retaining all five predictions of a sampled bottle. These are conditional intervals for the saved fitted predictions; they exclude refitting, repeated development and multiplicity adjustment. The dashed line denotes equal RMSE. Ten analytes show positive spectral gains with conditional intervals above zero, whereas ethyl lactate has no stable gain. The comparison quantifies added predictive value over this specified baseline; it does not establish molecular selectivity or superiority over every possible alcohol-strength model.

## Figure 2

Concentration-dependent coverage and direction of interval failure. (a) Empirical coverage of the spectral strategy selector's nominal 90% adaptive marginal prediction intervals. Filled circles indicate reference concentrations at or below the fitting-set 75th percentile (Q75); open squares indicate concentrations above that threshold. Connecting lines pair the two groups for the same analyte. The vertical dashed line marks nominal coverage. (b) For the high-concentration group, stacked bars separate records with the true concentration above the upper interval bound (blue) from records below the lower bound (pale gray); the total is the uncovered proportion. Groups are defined separately in every outer fold using fitting-set thresholds. Interval fitting uses 83 or 84 bottles and calibration uses 28 additional bottles; each test prediction remains outside both sets. High groups contain 168–182 prediction records from 39–43 distinct bottles, with group membership changing across repetitions. Percentages pool the saved records and are descriptive; repeated predictions of one bottle are correlated. Truth-defined groups are retrospective diagnostics. All eleven high groups have coverage below 90% and negative mean prediction bias. For ethyl lactate, 1-butanol and 1-propanol, every high-group miss lies above the upper prediction bound, revealing directional underestimation hidden by overall coverage.

## Figure 3

Value of selective review after controlling retained sample composition. (a) Macro normalized RMSE of the observed retention rule and two equal-count random controls at nominal retention fractions of 50%, 70%, 90% and 100%. The horizontal axis shows realized mean retention across the eleven analytes. The gray dashed curve is the mean of unstratified random policies; the orange dash-dot curve additionally matches retained counts within reference-concentration quartiles. Shading spans the 2.5th–97.5th percentiles of 2,000 random policies, rather than confidence limits for generalization error. Random counts are matched within each outer-fold/analyte evaluation; a bottle has a common random priority across repetitions. Concentration thresholds come from fitting data, while test reference values are used only for this retrospective matching diagnostic. At nominal 70% retention (69.48% realized), the observed macro error is 4.76% below the unstratified random mean but only 1.00% below the concentration-matched mean and lies within the latter distribution. (b) Analyte-specific gains versus the concentration-matched random mean at nominal 70% retention. Filled points are the observed gains; open circles mark the random mean (zero). Gray segments are the random-policy percentile ranges transformed to the same gain scale. Larger blue points identify acetaldehyde and ethyl acetate, whose observed errors are below the random-policy 2.5th percentiles. These descriptive comparisons have no multiplicity correction. Negative values indicate poorer retained-sample performance. Ordinary point-prediction results use a different fitting budget and should not be subtracted from these interval-mode errors.

## Figure 4

Aggregate accuracy across ten spectral prediction procedures. Points show the mean across eleven analytes of root mean squared prediction error normalized record-wise by the corresponding training-set interquartile range; lower values indicate smaller error. Each procedure is assessed using the same 139 bottles, five outer repetitions and five folds per repetition. Blue marks the prespecified spectral strategy selector, orange marks the original 69-candidate unanchored reference, and gray marks the other procedures from the expanded 138-candidate comparison. Direct labels report values to four decimal places. This is a point-prediction comparison using 111 or 112 training bottles per outer fold, without the calibration reserve needed for interval evaluation. Display order is descriptive. The expanded unanchored ensemble's numerically lowest aggregate error does not replace the prespecified primary procedure. Historical development used these same bottles, so the comparison represents retrospective internal evaluation. Small observed differences are not evidence of a stable improvement; the paired comparison with the strong original-library reference should guide interpretation.
